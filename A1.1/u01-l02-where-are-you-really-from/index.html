Where Are You Really From?

Big cities, small towns, and local neighborhoods. Your place, your country, and your friends.

01 · Warm-Up

Two Places: City or Town?

Look at the two places. Click your favorite place, and say 3 short lines with a partner.

1. Setup: Show the Origins Gallery (4–5 min) and ask students to click the place closer to their roots (small town/neighborhood vs. big city).
2. Modeling: Model your own answer using [P + N] only: "I'm from a big city. My hometown is Monterrey. Are you from this city?"
3. Check: Pair students for the 3 Quick Exchange Questions. Enforce boundary [F]: no come from with do, no action verbs (live, work), and no Wh- questions.
4. Keys: Open-ended personal responses; listen for accurate I'm from / My hometown is and rising intonation on Are you from...?
Option A · Quiet Place
A Small, Quiet Town

Small streets, nice people, and a quiet neighborhood.

Option B · Big Place
A Big, Busy City

Big buildings, busy streets, and many people.

3 Quick Questions

Say each line with a partner.

  • 1. "I'm from a ____ (big city / small town)."
  • 2. "My hometown is ____ (quiet / big / small / busy)."
  • 3. "Are you from this city?" → "Yes, I am." / "No, I'm not."

03 · USA Chunks

3 Useful USA Phrases

Use these 3 phrases as one piece.

1. Setup: Present these 3 expressions as ready-to-use conversational blocks [L].
2. Modeling: Play each Chunk and Example audio. Do NOT analyze grammar inside "Born and raised!".
3. Check: Quick choral repetition + have each student answer "Are you from [City/Town]? — Yes! Born and raised!" (remind them: never say *I'm born*).
4. Keys: All 3 chunks appear in the Roots & Real Places magazine profiles next.

04 · Reading

Three People, Three Places

Read about Mateo, Valeria & Sofía, and Daniel & Lucas.

1. Setup: Ask students to read the 3 profiles silently (4 min) and spot all 3 USA Chunks before answering the comprehension questions. Play the optional Reading audio (Listen (after reading)) only after silent reading.
2. Modeling: Point out the Yes/No questions in the text ("Are we from Madrid? No, we aren't." / "Are they from the same neighborhood? Yes, they are!") as inductive preview for Grammar.
3. Check: Students answer the 3 Comprehension Check questions individually, then justify each answer with a line from the text.
4. Keys: 1. B (No, he isn't — a small town.) · 2. C (No, they aren't.) · 3. A (Yes, they are — Chapinero.).
Profile 01 · Country & Town
Mateo
Oaxaca, Mexico

"Hi! I'm Mateo. I'm from Mexico—born and raised! My hometown isn't a big city. My hometown is a small town, out in the middle of nowhere! Is my neighborhood big and busy? No, it isn't. My neighborhood is small and quiet."

Profile 02 · Sisters & City
Valeria & Sofía
Guadalajara, Mexico

"I'm Valeria, and this is Sofía. Are we from Madrid? No, we aren't! We're from Guadalajara, a big city in Mexico—born and raised! Sofía is local, and she's very happy in Guadalajara."

Profile 03 · Old Friends
Daniel & Lucas
Bogotá, Colombia

"Daniel is from Colombia, and Lucas is from Colombia, too. Are they from the same neighborhood in Bogotá? Yes, they are! He's from Chapinero, and Lucas is from Chapinero too. 'Small world!'"

Check Your Reading

1. Is Mateo from a big, busy city?
2. Are Valeria and Sofía from Madrid?
3. Are Daniel and Lucas from the same neighborhood?
05 · Grammar

He / She / We / They + To Be & Yes/No Questions

Say where people are from. Put Is or Are first to ask a Yes/No question.

1. Setup: Connect back to the Reading profiles ("She's very happy", "Are we from Madrid? No, we aren't.", "Are they from the same neighborhood? Yes, they are.").
2. Modeling: Highlight subject–verb inversion for Yes/No questions (He is → Is he...? / They are → Are they...?) and remind students never to contract affirmative short answers ("Yes, she is."). Enforce [F]: no come from with do, no Wh-.
3. Check: Students complete the 4 Change the Sentence items (Sentence ↔ Yes/No Question) and read both versions aloud.
4. Keys: 1. Is she from Medellín? · 2. They aren't from a big city. / They're not from a big city. · 3. Are we from the same neighborhood? · 4. He is from a small town. / He's from a small town.

Use is / isn't with he / she. Use are / aren't with we / they. Put Is / Are first for a Yes/No question.

Grammar Practice

Change the Sentence

Write the question or sentence.

1. Make a question: "She is from Medellín."
2. Make a negative sentence (No): "Are they from a big city?"
3. Make a question: "We are from the same neighborhood."
4. Make a positive sentence (Yes): "Is he from a small town?"
06 · Speaking

Origin Mixer

Pick a card. Ask your partner Yes/No questions about their country, city, and neighborhood.

1. Setup: Assign each student an Origin Card (Card 1–4; in 1-on-1, you take Card 2 or 3). Students ask Yes/No questions to discover their partner's origin and any shared connection.
2. Modeling: Demo with one student: "Are you from Mexico? — Yes, I am! Born and raised in Guadalajara. — Small world! Is your friend Camila from Guadalajara too?"
3. Check: Monitor without interrupting; note any *Do you come from* or *He are* slips for delayed feedback.
4. Keys: Target evidence: accurate Are you / Is he / Is she / Are they questions, short answers, and 2+ USA Chunks.

07 · Exit Ticket

My 3 Sentences

Write 3 true sentences about you and a friend.

1. Setup: Give 3–4 minutes for independent writing without copying a model.
2. Modeling: Remind students to include: (1) where they are from (hometown / city / town / country), (2) one sentence with he / she / we / they + To Be, and (3) one Yes/No question.
3. Check: Students click Check My Sentences, read aloud, and self-verify the 4 Can-Do statements.
4. Keys: Understanding Comes Before Advancement: advance to A1.1-U01-L03 only when the student achieves E2 (3 accurate sentences + 2 chunks + Origin Mixer without come from + do, action verbs, or Wh-).

Write 3 Sentences

Use: (1) 1 place word (city, town, hometown, neighborhood, country, local), (2) he / she / we / they + is / are, and (3) 1 Yes/No question (e.g., Is she...? / Are they...?).
