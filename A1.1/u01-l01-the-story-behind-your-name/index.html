The Story Behind Your Name

First names, nicknames, and the personal stories behind what people call us when we meet for the first time.

01 · Warm-Up

Name Tag Dilemma

Vote on the quick poll and share your reaction with a partner.

1. Setup: Open the Name Tag Dilemma poll (4–5 min) and ask students to pick Option A, B, or C.
2. Modeling: Model your own answer first without action verbs: "I'm Daniel. My nickname is Dani. In class, call me Dani!"
3. Check: Pair students for the 3 Quick Exchange Questions. Enforce boundary [F]: do not introduce action verbs (like, prefer, use) or he/she yet.
4. Keys: Open-ended personal responses; listen for accurate I'm / My and clear pronunciation of names.

Quick Class Poll

On a coffee cup or a name tag, which name is better for you?

3 Quick Exchange Questions

Complete and say each line out loud with a partner.

  • 1. "My first name is ____. Happy or not happy with it?"
  • 2. "My nickname is ____."
  • 3. "In class, call me ____!"

Intelligibility Focus

I'm vs. I am

Listen to the rhythm. Use I'm in fast conversation, and I am for emphasis or at the end of a short answer ("Yes, I am.").

I'm /aɪm/ 1 smooth syllable: "I'm Sofía."
I am /aɪ ˈæm/ 2 clear syllables: "Yes, I am."

03 · USA Pragmatic Chunks

3 Natural Conversation Blocks

Use these 3 fixed expressions just as they are—no need to break them apart.

1. Setup: Present these 3 expressions as whole, ready-to-use conversational blocks [L].
2. Modeling: Play each Chunk and Example audio, emphasizing warm intonation. Do NOT analyze grammar inside "It's no big deal" or "Just call me...".
3. Check: Quick choral repetition + have each student say "Just call me [preferred name]".
4. Keys: All 3 chunks will reappear in the Listening dialogue next.

04 · Listening

The Name-Tag Confession

Listen to Sofi and Liam meet in person for the first time. Answer the 3 questions before opening the transcript.

1. Setup: Keep the transcript locked during the first 2 listens so students focus on real comprehension and notice I'm / I'm not / My in context before Grammar.
2. Modeling: Play the audio once for general gist (who are the speakers?), then a second time for the 3 comprehension items.
3. Check: Once all 3 items are answered correctly, unlock and read the transcript together, highlighting I'm not Sofi and Yes, I am.
4. Keys: 1. B (Elena) · 2. C ("It's no big deal! Just call me Sofi.") · 3. A (nickname).

05 · Grammar

I / you + To Be & Possessive My

Introduce yourself, correct a mix-up, and confirm someone else's name.

1. Setup: Connect back to the Listening transcript ("I'm not Sofi", "Yes, I am", "My first name is Sofía").
2. Modeling: Point out that I pairs with am ('m), you with are ('re), and My + noun uses is (or are for plural initials). Enforce [F]: no he/she/they or Are you...? inversion here.
3. Check: Students complete the 4 Spot & Fix cards individually, then read the corrected sentences aloud.
4. Keys: 1. I'm Mateo. · 2. My nickname is Leo. · 3. Yes, I am. · 4. I'm not Alex.

Use I'm / I'm not and You're / You're not to say who you are (or aren't), and pair My + a name word with is / isn't to share your name details.

Form I + To Be You + To Be Possessive My + Noun
Affirmative (+) I am / I'm
I'm Marco.		 You are / You're
You're Ana.		 My [name] is
My nickname is Leo.
Negative (−) I am not / I'm not
I'm not Marcos.		 You are not / You're not
You're not late.		 My [name] is not / isn't
My first name isn't Alex.

Quick confirmation note: In conversation, you can confirm a name with rising voice: "You're Sofía?" → "Yes, I am." / "No, I'm not."

06 · Speaking

1. Setup: Pair students (or play Partner B yourself in 1-on-1). Partner A only views Card A; Partner B only views Card B.
2. Modeling: Demo Round 1 with one student using the Useful Model Lines (combining P + N + L).
3. Check: Monitor without interrupting; note any *I'm is* or *I not* slips to review before clicking Switch to Round 2.
4. Keys: Target evidence: confirmation ("What's your name?" / "Are you...?"), correction ("I'm not... My nickname is..."), and 2+ USA Chunks.

07 · Exit Ticket

My 3-Sentence Name Profile

Write 3 true sentences about your name, then verify your measurable Can-Do evidence.

1. Setup: Give 3–4 minutes for independent writing without copying a model.
2. Modeling: Remind students of the 3 required elements: I'm / I'm not, My + name word, and Just call me...
3. Check: Students click Check My Profile and read their 3 sentences aloud, then self-check the Can-Do list.
4. Keys: Understanding Comes Before Advancement: advance to A1.1-U01-L02 only when the student achieves E2 (3 accurate sentences + 2 chunks + Information Gap without *I'm is* or *I not*).

Observable Micro-Production

Include: (1) I'm or I'm not, (2) My + a name word (first name, nickname, last name, initials), and (3) Just call me...
