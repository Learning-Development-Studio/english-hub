The First 30 Seconds

New people, quick questions, and real connections. Your name, your origin, and your job in half a minute.

01 · Warm-Up

30-Second Timeline

Look at the 3 moments in a first chat. Click 'Yes, easy!' or 'Not easy' for each moment, then talk with a partner.

1. Setup: Present the 30-Second Timeline (4–5 min). Ask students to click "Yes, easy!" or "Not easy" for each moment in a first chat (no percentages or scores).
2. Modeling: Model a 30-second intro using [P] (L01–L03 statements and Yes/No questions): "Hi! I'm Carlos. I'm from Puebla, and I'm a teacher. Are you a teacher too?"
3. Check: Pair students for the 3 Quick Questions using [P] (Are you...?). Enforce boundary [F]: NO do/does and NO action verbs (work, live, have, like, want).
4. Keys: Open-ended personal responses with Yes, I am! / No, I'm not and I'm...
0–10s · Name
Name

"Hi! I'm Sofía. Just call me Sofi."

10–20s · Origin
Origin

"I'm from Guadalajara—born and raised!"

20–30s · Job
Job

"I'm a designer. Are you a designer too?"

3 Quick Questions

Ask and answer with a partner.

  • 1. "Are you Sofi / Daniel?" → "Yes, I am! / No, I'm not. I'm ____."
  • 2. "Are you from a big city?" → "Yes, I am! / No, I'm not. I'm from ____."
  • 3. "Are you a student or in a job?" → "I'm a / an ____."

03 · USA Chunks

3 Useful USA Phrases

Use these 3 phrases as one piece in a quick chat.

1. Setup: Present these 3 expressions as ready-to-use conversational blocks [L].
2. Modeling: Play each Phrase and Example audio. Do NOT analyze grammar inside "Long time no see!" or "Good to know!".
3. Check: Quick choral repetition + mini-exchange: Teacher says "Long time no see! What's new?" and each student answers with 1 short To Be sentence ("I'm a designer now!") → Teacher reacts "Good to know!".
4. Keys: All 3 chunks appear in the 30-Second Profiles reading next.

04 · Reading

30-Second Profiles

Read about Clara, Diego, and Nadia & Sam. Then answer the 3 questions.

1. Setup: Ask students to read the 3 profiles silently (4 min) and spot all 3 USA Chunks before answering the comprehension questions. Play the optional Reading audio (Listen (after reading)) only after silent reading.
2. Modeling: Point out the Wh- questions in the profiles ("Who am I?", "Where are my parents from?", "How old am I?", "What's the job for Nadia and Sam?") as an inductive preview for Grammar.
3. Check: Students answer the 3 Comprehension Check questions individually, then justify each answer with a line from the profile.
4. Keys: 1. B (No, she isn't — she's a graphic designer.) · 2. C (No, he isn't — he's 31.) · 3. A (Yes, they are — engineers in Chicago.).

05 · Grammar

Wh- Questions + To Be

Ask for real information in the first 30 seconds with Who, What, Where, and How old.

1. Setup: Connect back to the Reading profiles ("Who is she?", "Where are my parents from?", "How old am I?", "What's the job for Nadia and Sam?").
2. Modeling: Highlight the word order: Wh- word + am / is / are + subject, then drill the Pronunciation Focus (What's /wʌts/ vs. Where's /wɛrz/ and falling intonation ↘ on Wh- questions). Enforce [F]: NEVER use do/does (e.g., *What do you do?* or *Where do you live?*).
3. Check: Students build the 4 questions in Wordbox / Sentence Reordering by clicking the word chips in order, then read each question aloud with falling intonation (↘).
4. Keys: 1. What's your name? · 2. Where is she from? · 3. Who is your friend? · 4. How old are you?

Put Who, What, Where, or How old first, then am / is / are.

Wh- Word Meaning Question (Wh- + To Be) Example Answer
Who A person Who is she? / Who are they? She's my partner, Elena.
What A thing, name, job, or contact What's your name? / What's your job? I'm Clara. I'm a designer.
Where A place Where are you from? / Where is he from? I'm from Lima, Peru.
How old Age (years) How old are you? / How old is he? I'm 28. / He's 31.
Pronunciation Focus

What's vs. Where's & Question Voice

Listen to What's and Where's. For Wh- questions, your voice goes down at the end.

What's /wʌts/ vs. Where's /wɛrz/ 1 fast sound each: What's your name? / Where's she from?
What's your name? ↘ · How old are you? ↘ Voice goes down on the last word.

06 · Speaking

Speed Networking (30-Second Round)

Pick a role card. Meet your partner for 30 seconds and ask 3 Wh- questions.

1. Setup: Assign each student a Role Card (Card 1–4; in 1-on-1, you take Card 2 or 3). Students run 30-second speed-networking rounds.
2. Modeling: Demo with one student: "Long time no see! What's new? — My new job! I'm a designer. — Good to know! Where are you from?"
3. Check: Monitor without interrupting; note any *What do you do?* or *Where do you live?* slips for delayed feedback.
4. Keys: Target evidence: accurate What's / Where / Who / How old questions, 3+ contact words, and 2+ USA Chunks.

07 · Exit Ticket

My 30-Second Intro

Write 2 short sentences about you and 2 Wh- questions for a new partner.

1. Setup: Give 3–4 minutes for independent writing in the blank textarea without copying a model.
2. Modeling: Remind students to include: (1) 2 sentences about themselves (name, origin, age, or job), and (2) 2 Wh- questions with To Be.
3. Check: Students click Check My Intro, read aloud, and self-verify the 3 Can-Do statements.
4. Keys: Understanding Comes Before Advancement: advance to A1.1-U02-L01 only when the student achieves E2 (2 accurate sentences + 2 Wh- questions + Speed Networking without do/does or action verbs).

Write Your 30-Second Intro

Include: (1) your name, origin, age, or job, and (2) 2 Wh- questions (Who, What, Where, How old + is / are).
