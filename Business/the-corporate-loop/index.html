Phase 01 · Engage & Discover

The Mintzberg Reality Check

Textbooks portray business as calm planning and polite meetings. Which of these operational disruptions did you or your department face this week?

The Organizational Truth: You just described what Dr. Henry Mintzberg discovered in his landmark research: managerial life is never a calm, predictable sequence. It is a relentless, fragmented loop of brief interruptions and reactive negotiations. You cannot survive in global business with generic English; you need the exact language that destrabs the loop.

Phase 03 · Guided Discovery

Linguistic Anchor: Causality & Diplomatic Stance

Compare how two different Mexican executives responded to this exact crisis. Observe the grammatical structure and the impact on the partnership:

Version A · Clumsy & Accusatory

“Thanks to the customs delay, the shipment is late. You made a strict schedule, so please explain me what we do.”

  • Thanks to: Sounds sarcastic/inappropriate for negative operational failures.
  • You made: Aggressive finger-pointing damages executive goodwill.
  • Explain me: Serious fossil prepositional error (requires explain to me).
Version B · Executive Standard (Diplomatic & Precise)

“Due to an unforeseen customs audit, road transit was halted. Consequently, an emergency air charter was arranged to protect your assembly timeline.”

  • Due to + Noun Phrase: Objective, non-emotional causality.
  • Depersonalized Passive Voice: (was halted, was arranged) focuses on the solution, not personal blame.
  • Consequently: Executive discourse connector linking cause to immediate mitigation.
Discovery Takeaway: In executive English, due to is followed by a nominalized noun phrase (due to an unforeseen audit), never a full clause. To preserve commercial trust, high-level executives use depersonalized passive structures to address operational failure without attacking the counterparty.

Phase 05 · Perform & Apply

Executive Mission: Break the Loop

Apply your learning to solve the Horizon Mobility crisis. Your response will generate individual observable evidence.

The Scenario Mandate
Your Role: VP of Operations at Horizon Mobility.
Recipient: Megan Stone (Client Director, Detroit).
Your Task: Write a 2-to-3 sentence executive response to Megan's voicemail. You must:
1. State the root cause using due to or as a result of.
2. Maintain a depersonalized, diplomatic stance (no finger-pointing).
3. Propose an actionable contingency (e.g. an emergency air charter) using consequently or provided that.

Faculty Pedagogical Guide (Documento Rector Secc. 3)

1. Canonical Alignment & Target Outcome

Course: Executive Business English Track · BE-00 Orientation.
Outcome: Students articulate causality and systemic operational disruption using objective markers (due to + NP, consequently) and depersonalized passive voice, reframing the corporate lifecycle through Mintzberg and Senge.

2. Language Control (P / P? / N / F)

  • P (Previous): B1 core grammar (Past Simple, Present Continuous, basic conjunctions *because*, *so*).
  • N (New - Taught Here): Due to + nominalized noun phrase, consequently, depersonalized passive hedging (was halted, appears to omit).
  • F (Future / Beyond Scope): Advanced third conditionals or complex legal force majeure clauses (not tested as traps).

3. Qualitative Assessment Rubric (E0 – E3)

  • E0: Output fails to address causality or uses accusatory/fossil forms despite prompts.
  • E1: Logically understandable but relies on raw literal translation (*"thanks to the delay we have problems"*).
  • E2 (Proficient): Accurately deploys *due to + NP* and passive voice, providing a clear contingency solution.
  • E3 (Exemplary): Fluid, executive-level phrasing demonstrating high diplomatic tact and seamless discourse linking.
