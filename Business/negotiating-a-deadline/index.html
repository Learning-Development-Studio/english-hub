Phase 2 · Executive Lexicon

Supply Chain & Corporate Jargon Decoder

Master the 4 critical terms used by global supply chain executives in crisis negotiations.

Lead Time
The latency or total period elapsed from order confirmation to physical delivery at the client's dock.
"Our standard lead time for semiconductor fabrication is six weeks."
Bottleneck
A congestion point or narrow stage in a process that throttles the overall flow and slows down production.
"Customs inspection at the port created a severe bottleneck for all inbound freight."
Expedite
To accelerate the processing or transportation of goods by paying premium priority rates (e.g., air charter).
"If we expedite the critical parts, we will avoid shutting down the plant."
Backlog
An accumulation of uncompleted orders or pending work that must be resolved before taking new requests.
"The typhoon left a three-week backlog of shipping containers at the dock."
Liquidated Damages (SLA Penalty)
Predetermined, contractually stipulated compensation that one party must pay if they fail to meet delivery deadlines or performance agreements.
"Clause 14 states liquidated damages of $50,000 per 24 hours of unauthorized plant shutdown."

Phase 3 · Grammar Anchor

First Conditional & Fossilized Error Buster

Never say "If we will expedite..." again. Master the conditional syntax of boardroom commitments.

The Corporate Formula: Real Contingency Planning

Use the First Conditional to propose real, actionable contingency terms to clients.

IF + PRESENT SIMPLE (Condition) → WILL + BASE VERB (Guaranteed Result)
THE FOSSILIZED SPANISH ERROR (DO NOT SAY THIS IN FRONT OF THE BOARD):

✗ "If we will expedite the batch, we will arrive on Friday."
✓ "If we expedite the batch, we will arrive on Friday."

Why it happens: In Spanish, native speakers naturally use the future subjunctive or duplicate future intent ("Si aceleraremos..."). In English, WILL is strictly prohibited inside the IF clause.

Phase 5 · Executive Writing

BLUF Architecture: Formal Written Agreement

Draft the binding confirmation email. In the corporate world, if it isn't documented in writing, it didn't happen.

The BLUF Framework (Bottom Line Up Front): C-suite executives and procurement directors scan emails on their phones between meetings. Never bury the solution at the end. State the bottom-line agreement in Sentence 1.
