Workshop Context & Diagnostic

Why saying "yes" to everything leads to organizational friction, burnout, and diminished executive authority.

The Chronic Corporate Trap

Many professionals believe that saying "no" will make them seem uncooperative, lazy, or incapable of advancement. As a result, they overcommit, work through weekends, and produce rushed results that harm strategic outcomes.

The Executive Leadership Shift

Setting boundaries is not about refusal; it is about protecting value. Clear boundaries communicate that your time is governed by intentional strategic priorities rather than reactive emergencies.

Nedra Glover Tawwab · 3-Step Guide to Better Boundaries at Work

Watch the video and examine the 3 pillars of professional boundary setting.

The 3 Core Pillars

1

Be Clear, Direct, and Unapologetic

Do not hedge with excessive apologies like "I'm so terribly sorry to bother you, but maybe...". State what you can and cannot do with calm, level authority. Clarity is kindness.

2

Do Not Over-Explain or Justify

Over-explaining invites negotiation. When you give five personal reasons why you cannot take a task, the other person attempts to solve each reason. State your capacity limit simply.

3

Offer a Strategic Alternative (Trade-Off)

Show that you care about organizational success by framing the boundary around trade-offs: "If this new task takes priority, we will need to postpone the client review to Tuesday. Does that align with your goal?"

The Executive Refusal Toolkit

Memorize and deploy these high-impact diplomatic phrases in everyday workplace interactions.

Bandwidth & Current Commitment
"I would love to support this, but my current bandwidth is fully committed to the Q4 audit through Thursday."
Use when: A colleague or manager asks for urgent help on a project outside your scope.
Strategic Trade-Off (Prioritization)
"I can take this on, but to do it properly, we will need to deprioritize the onboarding deck. Which of the two is your higher priority?"
Use when: Your direct manager assigns an extra project without removing existing deliverables.
After-Hours / Weekend Protection
"I am logging off for the evening. I will review this first thing tomorrow morning at 8:30 a.m."
Use when: Emails, Slacks, or WhatsApp messages arrive late in the evening without emergency status.
Meeting Fatigue & Focus Time
"I am in deep focus time working on the budget model. Could you please send a quick summary via email so I can provide feedback asynchronously?"
Use when: Someone invites you to a 45-minute sync that could easily be handled in two sentences.

High-Stakes Workplace Roleplay Scenarios

Practice these authentic corporate situations with your instructor and peers.

Scenario A · The Friday 4:55 PM Request Manager vs Specialist

Context: It is Friday afternoon at 4:55 PM. Your director sends a Slack message: "Hey, can you quickly draft this 10-slide competitive benchmark before Monday 8:00 AM? It shouldn't take more than an hour or two."

Your Goal: Acknowledge receipt, decline to work over the weekend, and establish a realistic delivery window on Monday afternoon.
Scenario B · The Client Scope Creep Account Lead vs Enterprise Client

Context: A client calls asking for three additional custom reports that were not included in the signed Statement of Work (SOW): "We really need these extra breakdowns for our board meeting next week. You guys can just throw that in, right?"

Your Goal: Remain warm and collaborative, explain that this falls outside the active scope, and offer a change-order quote or a future phase.
Scenario C · The "Help Me Real Quick" Colleague Peer vs Peer

Context: A teammate who is consistently disorganized approaches your desk: "Can you look over my entire spreadsheet and fix the formulas? I'm completely behind schedule!"

Your Goal: Protect your own deadline without humiliating your peer. Offer 15 minutes of guidance after your primary deliverable is submitted.
