Teacher plan · games first

Yesterday on trial. Was or were?

Odd-sentence traps, grammar hits, three Practice drills, then TOEFL-style Reading/Listening MCQ and a Lost-weekend role-play — no written reflection in Speaking.

≈ 55–60 min 1:1 or small group Notice → Grammar → Practice Trap hunt · short answers · role-play

Aim

Use was / were / wasn’t / weren’t in statements, questions, and short answers about yesterday — and repair classic traps (He were, They was, Was you…?).

  • Can-do: Talk about yesterday with clear was/were choices.
  • Materials: Student page + Guess Who game + phone/laptop; board or paper for odd-sentence sets.

0 · Warm-up 5–7 min Game

Odd Sentence Out (was / were) — activate the past of be before the page.

  1. Write / say three sentences. Two are correct; one is a trap.
  2. Student spots the odd one, says why, and repairs it.
  3. Run 4–5 rounds fast. Mix subjects: I / she / you / they.
Starter sets:
A) She was tired. · He were late. · It was cold.
B) They were at home. · We wasn’t hungry. · You were early.
C) Was you at school? · Was Nora free? · Were they busy?
D) I wasn’t there. · They was happy. · We weren’t late.
Line: “Yesterday is on trial. Find the sentence that breaks the was/were rule — then fix it.”
Optional score: +1 You per clean repair

0b · Guess Who · Famous people 8–12 min Game

Interactive board game that forces full Was he / Was she…? questions and short answers (Yes, she was. / No, he wasn’t.).

  1. Open the live game (projector or phones). Mode: Solo vs computer, or Class / 2 players.
  2. Students must say the full question aloud before tapping Ask.
  3. Eliminate cards from Yes/No answers, then Guess. Optional trap fix after a win.

Play Guess Who · Famous people

Line: “Build only was/were questions. No free typing — tap the chips, say it out loud, then Ask.”

1 · Notice 6–7 min Notice

Open 1 Notice. Observe only — no “why” yet.

  1. Student reads each snapshot and taps was / were (or wasn’t / Were…).
  2. You coach with one prompt only: “Who is the sentence about?” — do not ask them to explain the rule.
  3. Quick oral: they point to three subjects on the page and say only the matching form (was or were).
Save the rule for Grammar next. Notice = classify; Grammar = teach rules; Practice = drills.

2 · Grammar hit 1 7 min Grammar Board split

Teach rules only — no analysis writing on the student page.

was

I · he · she · it
I was tired. · She wasn’t at home.

were

you · we · they
You were late. · They weren’t hungry.

Fire round: You throw a subject pronoun; they answer only was or were — 12 flash subjects, no thinking time.

3 · Grammar hit 2 7–8 min Grammar Short-answer duel

Questions + short answers (still oral — page Grammar has the map).

  • Was I / he / she / it …? → Yes, … was. / No, … wasn’t.
  • Were you / we / they …? → Yes, … were. / No, … weren’t.
  1. Model two questions from the Grammar tab.
  2. Short-answer duel: you ask 8 Was/Were questions about yesterday; answers must be short forms only.
Trap call-out: Slip in Was you…? once — they must reject it and rebuild with Were you…?

4 · Grammar hit 3 5–6 min Grammar Trap hunt

Classic traps on the page (wrong/right cards only).

  1. Show wrong/right: He were / They was / Was you…?
  2. Student repairs each out loud (subject → form).
  3. Quick oral: they invent one trap for you to fix (role reversal).

5 · Practice drills 12–14 min Practice Stations

Three accordion drills with Check — not open-ended analyse writing.

  1. Open 3 Practice. Run stations:
  • 1st drill — Choose was / were / wasn’t / weren’t (gap MCQ)
  • 2nd drill — Fix the trap (choose correct rewrite)
  • 3rd drill — Questions + short answers (MCQ)
  1. After each Check: one miss → they read the why out loud.
  2. Cool-down: Reading (Open Day story + was/were taps + TOEFL MCQ) OR Listening (Diego/Lucia audio; script toggle stays below the player).

6 · Produce 8–10 min Speaking / Writing

Get language out — Speaking is oral only.

  1. Speaking · Lost weekend role-play: Student A asks Was/Were questions; Student B answers with short form + one detail. Switch roles. Then Mystery boxes if time.
  2. Writing tab: 6–8 lines about yesterday with mixed was/were + one question + one short answer — or finish for homework. No writing textarea inside Speaking.

Exit ticket 2 min

  1. Repair one trap on a sticky / chat: They was busy.
  2. Say one true was sentence and one true were sentence about yesterday.

Timing

Warm-up 5–7 · Notice 6–7 · Grammar 1–3 ≈ 20 · Practice 12–14 · Produce 8–10 · Exit 2 → ~55–60 min. Cut Grammar hit 3 oral invent if short.
