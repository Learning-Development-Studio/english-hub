Phase 02 · Notice & Contextualize

The Value Contrast: Emotional Pitch vs. Executive Justification

Compare these two 30-second pitches delivered during a mid-year 1-on-1. Observe how grammar and tone fundamentally alter authority:

Pitch A · Emotional & Task-Focused

“I work very hard every day and I always do what you ask. Everything is more expensive now, and I think I deserve a raise because I haven't received one in two years.”

  • Subjective focus: "I work hard" provides zero measurable business evidence.
  • Passive posture: "I do what you ask" positions you as a task-doer, not a strategic contributor.
  • Weak leverage: Relies on personal entitlement rather than ROI.
Pitch B · Executive Value Standard

“In Q1, I restructured our vendor onboarding workflow (Past Simple). Over the past six months, this initiative has reduced cycle times by 22% and has generated $35,000 in operational savings (Present Perfect). Given this expanded scope and tangible impact, I would like to schedule a conversation to align my compensation with my current level of responsibility.”

Click to listen to Pitch B cadence (30s)
  • Past Simple: Anchors the specific leadership initiative to a clear historical date (*In Q1, I restructured*).
  • Present Perfect: Proves the ongoing, cumulative value benefiting the company today (*has reduced, has generated*).
  • Professional closure: Requests an alignment based on expanded scope (*warrants an alignment*).
Phase 03 · Guided Grammar Discovery

The Time-Impact Formula: Past Simple vs. Present Perfect

How native executives structure their track record. Notice how the verb tenses divide the narrative into the initiative and the ongoing return:

Step 1 · The Past Milestone
Past Simple (Finished Date)
Identifies the exact initiative you spearheaded.
“In March, I implemented a new ticketing protocol...”
→
Step 2 · The Cumulative ROI
Present Perfect (Ongoing Impact)
Quantifies the value created up to the present.
“...which has decreased ticket backlog by 35%...”
→
Step 3 · The Diplomatic Ask
Target Formal Request
Connects the proven value to role/compensation.
“...which warrants aligning my compensation with my scope.”
Inductive Rule: Use Past Simple when you cite a closed timeframe (In Q2, Last November, When I took over the team). Switch immediately to Present Perfect with duration adverbs (Over the past six months, Since then, To date) to prove that the company is currently harvesting the financial and operational rewards of your work.

The Value Pitch Builder (Individual Evidence)

Draft your own 60-to-90-second Value Justification Pitch based on your actual role (or an assigned director role). Your submission will be evaluated against the institutional E0–E3 criteria.

Your Pitch Must Include:
1. One Specific Milestone (Past Simple): State what initiative or responsibility you took on with a past date (In Q1, Last fall, When I joined the project...).
2. Two Cumulative Impacts (Present Perfect): State how this initiative has improved, has reduced, has saved, or has generated measurable business results.
3. One Diplomatic Closing Request: Ask for a formal alignment of compensation or title (warrants a review of my compensation, explore aligning my title...).

Faculty Pedagogical Guide (Documento Rector Secc. 3)

1. Lesson Blueprint & Curricular Target

Course: Executive Business English Specialization.
Outcome: Learners defend quantifiable business value in high-stakes 1-on-1 reviews, using Past Simple for specific initiatives and Present Perfect for ongoing ROI, concluding with diplomatic negotiation formulas.

2. Language Control (P / P? / N / F)

  • P (Previous): Past Simple with time anchors (*in Q1, last month*), basic office communication.
  • P? (Check/Refine): Metric expressions (*reduced by 15%*, *saved 30 hours*).
  • N (New - Taught Here): Contrast of Past Simple vs. Present Perfect for ROI narration, collocations: *warrant a review, tangible impact, expanded scope, align compensation*.
  • F (Future): Complex mixed conditionals (*Had I not done X, we wouldn't have Y*), hostile ultimatums (excluded).

3. Qualitative Assessment Rubric (E0 – E3)

  • E0: Pitch relies on emotional/personal need with no quantifiable metrics or target verb contrast.
  • E1 (Emergent): Includes achievements but mixes verb tenses incorrectly (*"I have restructured in Q1"*).
  • E2 (Proficient): Flawlessly contrasts Past Simple milestone with Present Perfect ROI and includes a professional closing request.
  • E3 (Exemplary): Sophisticated executive cadence, seamless metrics integration, and diplomatic assertiveness.
