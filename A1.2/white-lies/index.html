3. Grammar Mechanics & -ing Spelling Lab

The Present Continuous formula and the 4 critical spelling rules for -ing.

Grammar Workbench
Structure: Subject + am / is / are (not) + Verb + -ing = Action occurring RIGHT NOW

The 4 Essential Spelling Rules of -ing

Rule 1 · General

Add -ing.

walk → walking
sleep → sleeping
cook → cooking
Rule 2 · Silent -e

Drop silent -e, add -ing.

drive → driving
leave → leaving
write → writing
Rule 3 · C-V-C Double

Consonant-Vowel-Consonant: double last letter.

run → running
sit → sitting
stop → stopping
Rule 4 · -ie to -y

Change -ie to -y, add -ing.

lie (tell a lie) → lying
tie → tying
die → dying

5. Spoken American Chunks: Real Functional Language

These prefabricated chunks are used every single day in the United States to handle delays and interruptions.

Functional Language [L]
"I'm on my way!"
Standard reply when someone asks where you are.
"Don't leave yet, I'm on my way!"
"Be right there!"
Expresses that your arrival is only a few seconds away.
"Almost at the door — be right there!"
"Caught red-handed!"
Used when someone discovers you doing something you denied.
"You said you were studying, but you're watching TV! Caught red-handed!"
"Yeah, right!"
Sarcastic reaction meaning "I do not believe you at all."
"You're driving? Yeah, right! I hear your dog barking!"
"Hold that thought!"
Polite pause when a real-time event interrupts your talk.
"Hold that thought — my boss is calling me right now."
"Traffic is a nightmare."
Universal real-time excuse for delays on the road.
"I'm crawling at 5 mph, traffic is an absolute nightmare."
