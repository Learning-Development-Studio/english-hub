Situational Vocabulary: Calls, Delays & Excuses

Target words for real-life phone situations and excuses at level A1.2.

stuck adjective
/stʌk/
Unable to move, escape, or make progress.
"I am stuck in traffic right now!"
traffic jam noun
/ˈtræf.ɪk dʒæm/
A long line of cars and trucks unable to move.
"There is a terrible traffic jam on 5th Avenue."
low battery noun phrase
/loʊ ˈbæt̬.ɚ.i/
When your phone has almost zero electrical energy.
"I must hang up, my phone has low battery!"
excuse noun
/ɪkˈskjuːs/
A reason you invent to explain why you are late.
"That is not a real reason, that is just an excuse."
background noise noun phrase
/ˈbæk.ɡraʊnd nɔɪz/
Sounds heard behind your voice during a call.
"What is that background noise? Are you in a diner?"
in a hurry phrase
/ɪn ə ˈhɝː.i/
Acting or moving quickly because time is short.
"I can't talk right now, I am in a big hurry!"
pajamas noun
/pəˈdʒɑː.məz/
Comfortable clothes worn specifically for sleeping.
"He claims he is ready, but he is still wearing pajamas."
emergency noun
/ɪˈmɝː.dʒən.si/
A sudden urgent event requiring immediate action.
"I have a quick emergency at the office."

Formal Grammar Clinic · Find and Fix the Classical A1.2 Errors

1. "He is drive to work right now."
Error: Main verb missing -ing ending.
2. "They sleeping in bed at this moment."
Error: Missing auxiliary verb BE (are).

Screen 05 · Functional Language

Spoken American Chunks

Prefabricated conversational units used daily across the US to manage delays and excuses.

"I'm on my way!"

Standard reply when someone asks for your current location.

"Don't cancel the table, I'm on my way right now!"
"Be right there!"

Signals your physical arrival will occur in moments.

"Just parking the car — be right there!"
"Caught red-handed!"

Used when catching someone in an obvious lie.

"You said you were studying? Caught red-handed!"
"Yeah, right!"

Sarcastic reaction meaning "I do not believe you at all."

"You're driving? Yeah, right! I hear TV sounds!"
"Hold that thought!"

Polite interruption to pause talk when someone calls.

"Hold that thought — someone is calling my cell."
"Traffic is a nightmare."

Universal excuse for vehicular delays in the city.

"I'm moving at 2 mph, traffic is an absolute nightmare."
