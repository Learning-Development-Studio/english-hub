Module 02 · Thematic Lexis

Time Markers: The Traffic Lights of English

In English, time markers dictate which verb tense you must choose. Never start with grammar rules—look at the time marker first!

🟢 Routine Markers → Present Simple

Use these when an action happens regularly, always, or repeatedly:

usually normally every day always on Mondays on weekends never

Example: “I usually cook healthy food on Mondays.”

🔴 Crisis / Temporary Markers → Present Continuous

Use these when an action is happening right now or is a short-term crisis:

this week this month today right now at the moment currently nowadays

Example: “However, this week I am living on fast food.”

Module 03 · Structural Precision

The Two Worlds & The State Verbs Shield

Master the exact formulas and protect yourself against the #1 fossilized error in English: applying continuous forms to state verbs.

Formula 1: Present Simple
Subject + Base Verb (-s / -es)

Negative: don't / doesn't + base verb

  • I work from 9 to 5.
  • Elena cooks healthy meals.
  • We don't stay late at the office.
Formula 2: Present Continuous
Subject + am / is / are + Verb-ING

Negative: am not / isn't / aren't + -ing

  • This week, I am working overtime.
  • Elena is eating instant noodles today.
  • They are not sleeping enough.

🛡️ THE STATE VERBS SHIELD (STRICT L&D STUDIO RULE)

Some verbs describe mental states, desires, knowledge, and feelings—NOT physical actions. Even if you are in the middle of a massive crisis, NEVER add -ing to these verbs:

need want know understand like hate
❌ Wrong: “Right now I am needing a coffee.”
✅ Correct: “Right now I need a coffee.”

Module 05 · Functional Language

USA Pragmatic Chunks: Empathy in Crisis Mode

When Americans see a friend or colleague overwhelmed by temporary stress, they use these exact idiomatic formulas.

Chunk 01 · Reassurance
“It's just a phase.”
This crazy situation is temporary. It will pass soon, so do not panic.
“Don't worry about the long hours. It's just a phase!”
Chunk 02 · Encouragement
“Hang in there!”
Be strong! Don't quit! You have the strength to survive this difficult moment.
“I know you are working 12 hours today. Hang in there!”
Chunk 03 · State of Health
“Under the weather.”
Feeling exhausted, slightly sick, drained of energy, or under physical strain.
“I feel a bit under the weather, so I am resting today.”

Crisis Dialogue Practice:

Coworker A: “Hey! How are you holding up? You look totally exhausted!”
Coworker B: “I am! I usually leave work at 5:00 PM, but this week I am working until 10:00 PM for the system audit. And to be honest, I feel a bit under the weather.”
Coworker A: “Oh no! Hang in there! The audit ends on Friday. It's just a phase!”
Coworker B: “Thanks, I really need to hear that.”
