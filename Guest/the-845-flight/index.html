Chapter two · Your language toolkit

Words that unlock decisions

Language studio

Find the words. Unlock the options.

Get the language you need to respond to a professional disruption. Then use it to make real decisions.

YOUR TOOLKIT

Before you investigate, equip yourself with the language of unexpected changes. Explore each expression, listen to its pronunciation, and decide where you could use it.

rescheduleverb

Change an appointment or meeting to a different time.

Could we reschedule for the afternoon?

availableadjective

Free, possible or ready to use.

Is another flight available?

alternativenoun

A different option when the original plan does not work.

We need an alternative.

confirmverb

Check or state that information is certain.

Can you confirm the time?

delaynoun / verb

A situation where something happens later than planned.

There is a delay of one hour.

prioritynoun

The thing that matters most right now.

Our priority is the client meeting.

proposalnoun

A plan or idea offered for consideration.

Our proposal includes three options.

reliableadjective

Something you can depend on.

We need a reliable connection.

Use the tools, don't memorize a list

Choose two expressions and explain how they could help in this situation. Then try saying one idea more professionally.

There is no single sentence everyone must reproduce.

Chapter six · Communication discovery

You've already made a decision. Can you make it convincing?

Communication discovery

Make your recommendation count.

Your answer is only the beginning. Add a useful detail, explain your reason, and move the conversation forward.

ANSWER · DETAIL · REASON

A client needs more than a short answer. Your challenge is to make your recommendation clear, useful and convincing—without changing what you really think.

Start with a real problem

Two messages. Which one helps the client more?

Message A

“We should meet online.”

Message B

“I think we should meet online. I can share the proposal on screen. Our replacement flight arrives after the meeting starts.”

Both messages give a recommendation. What extra information does the second message offer? Would you trust it more? Why?

Discover the communication formula

Answer → Detail → Reason

It's not about giving three different answers. It's about developing one meaningful response.

1. ANSWER

“I think we should meet online.”

Give your main idea or recommendation.

2. DETAIL

“I can share the proposal on screen.”

Add useful, specific information about your plan.

3. REASON

“Our replacement flight arrives after the meeting starts.”

Explain why your recommendation makes sense.

“I think we should meet online. I can share the proposal on screen. Our replacement flight arrives after the meeting starts.”

A reason does not always need to begin with because. Natural professional communication can use several linked sentences.

Try it your way

Build one complete professional response

Select ideas below to see how the three parts work together. Then create your own response based on the decisions you made earlier.

YOUR DRAFT

I recommend meeting online. We can share the proposal and discuss it together. The replacement flight arrives too late.

Choose combinations that make sense together. A logical explanation matters more than copying a model.

Optional · Extend the conversation

Ask a follow-up question

Answer → Detail → Reason helps you develop your response. A follow-up question is a separate skill: it keeps the discussion moving when you need confirmation or more information.

“Would an online meeting work for you?”

Check whether your proposal is acceptable.

“Could you confirm who will attend?”

Request information that affects your plan.

Language close-up

One small choice can change your tone

“We can meet online.”

Presents an available option.

“We could meet online.”

Offers a softer suggestion.

Can + base verb

We can meet.

Ability or practical possibility.

Could + base verb

We could meet.

A tentative suggestion.

Could we + base verb?

Could we meet?

A polite question or proposal.

Say “could we meet”, not “could we to meet”. You can use any language that communicates your idea accurately.
